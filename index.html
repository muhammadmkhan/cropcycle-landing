The gap

Three sides of agriculture, none of them talking to each other.

Pakistan's agriculture sector is the backbone of the economy — but the capital, trust and information needed to run it efficiently haven't caught up.

Farmers

Locked out of affordable financing

Nearly all Pakistani farms are smallholdings, and most sit outside the reach of formal bank credit — pushing them toward informal lenders instead.

65–80% typical effective annual cost of informal input credit
Investors

No accessible way into agriculture

Agriculture drives close to a quarter of the national economy, yet retail and diaspora capital has almost no structured, transparent way to fund it directly.

23.5% of Pakistan's GDP, largely closed to outside capital
Buyers

Sourcing is manual and unverifiable

Grade, provenance and delivery timing are negotiated by phone and trust, cycle after cycle — with no standard record of where produce actually came from.

0 shared verification standard across mandi supply chains
The mechanism

One crop cycle, three parties, one settlement.

Every cycle moves through the same three phases — the same states the platform tracks behind the scenes, from funding to final settlement.

01

Fund a cycle

An investor commits capital to a specific, verified crop cycle. Funds move into escrow and stay there — insurance is attached before the cycle goes live.

02

Grow, verified

The farmer executes against a simple task list, uploading geo-tagged photos and videos at each milestone. An agronomist reviews and clears each one.

03

Harvest & settle

Produce moves to a verified buyer through the marketplace or a standing contract. Escrow releases automatically: farmer paid, investor gets principal plus return, insurance triggers only if it's needed.

Trust & security

Built so that trust doesn't rely on anyone's word.

Every cycle carries its own paper trail — automatically, not on request.

Escrow payments

Investor capital is held by the platform and released in tranches, only against verified milestones.

Farmer verification

Farmers pass a KYC and land/plot review before any cycle they run can be funded.

Crop insurance

A pooled insurance fund, accrued from platform fees, backstops weather and yield shocks.

Geo-tagged milestones

Photo and video proof is timestamped and location-tagged, with anomaly detection on the media itself.

Business model

Revenue tied to real volume moving through the platform.

Four lines, all downstream of cycles that actually fund and settle.

StreamHow it worksTake rate
Cycle fee A platform fee on funded cycle value, paid at settlement by the investor and/or farmer. 2–4%
Marketplace margin A transaction fee on buyer orders and a margin on subscription boxes. 5–8%

About the founder

Building the financial infrastructure layer for agriculture.

MM
Muhammad Musa
Founder & CEO

Muhammad is a fintech and banking technology professional with 5+ years of experience across enterprise banking and payment systems. That background shaped CropCycle's core bet: agriculture doesn't need another marketplace app, it needs the same rigor — escrow, verification, reconciliation, audit trails — that banking and payments took decades to build.

CropCycle applies that infrastructure to crop cycles: every rupee is tracked from investor to escrow to farmer to buyer to settlement, with a state machine behind it instead of a spreadsheet.

"Farmers have always managed risk. What they've never had is a financial system built to price it fairly."
